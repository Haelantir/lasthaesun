Can I Ignore a Faucet That Sputters After the Water Was Turned Back On?
  Water was shut off for repair work or a utility outage, then turned back on, and now a faucet spits and sputters. Reader wants to know whether this is just trapped air working its way out (self-resolving) or a sign something is wrong. Separate "clears up within a few minutes of running" from "still sputtering after several minutes" or "only one fixture in the house does it."

Can I Ignore a Steering Wheel That Sits Crooked When the Car Drives Straight?
  The steering wheel's resting position (spoke alignment) is visibly off-center, but the car tracks straight with no pulling. This is specifically about the wheel's position after work like an alignment, tire replacement, or steering-component service — not a wheel with play in it, and not a whining noise while turning.

Can I Ignore Milky Residue Under the Oil Cap?
  Whitish, mayonnaise-like buildup is found under the oil filler cap when checking the oil. The reader's fear is a blown head gasket mixing coolant into the oil. Key distinguishing scenarios: residue confined to the underside of the cap vs. milky color on the dipstick itself, coolant level dropping or not, and a pattern of short cold-weather trips vs. not.

Can I Ignore a Laptop Charger That Makes a Faint Buzzing Noise?
  The charger charges the laptop normally but emits a quiet, consistent buzzing or coil-whine sound. Separate this faint/steady case from a charger that is loud, crackling, or hot to the touch — those point to a different verdict.
