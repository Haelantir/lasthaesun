# Mobility batch — opening motorcycles, bicycles, e-bikes and scooters.
# Cars already carry 18 of the 19 mobility pages, so this batch is deliberately
# 2 cars + 6 motorcycles + 5 bicycles + 4 e-bikes + 3 e-scooters.
# Every slug below is reserved as a draft in src/content/problems/planned-*.ts.

Can I Ignore a Battery Warning Light?
  The dashboard battery/charging light, not a hybrid or EV traction battery
  warning. Separate the cases that actually change the answer: the light comes
  on and goes off, it stays on while driving, it comes on with dimming lights or
  a slowing blower, it comes on together with a temperature warning. This is a
  charging-system warning, so the question of how far you get is about what runs
  the car once the alternator stops contributing.

Can I Ignore Blue Smoke From My Exhaust?
  Blue or blue-grey smoke, which is a different question from the white vapour
  page the site already has. Worth separating: smoke only on a cold start, smoke
  on hard acceleration, smoke when lifting off after a long downhill, constant
  smoke at every speed. Turbocharged and older high-mileage engines behave
  differently here.

Can I Ignore a Loose Motorcycle Chain?
  Drive chain slack outside the manufacturer's range. Separate slack that is
  merely at the loose end of spec, slack well beyond it, uneven slack that
  changes as the wheel turns, and a chain that has gone slack again days after
  it was adjusted. The owner's manual figure is the reference point.

Can I Ignore an Oil Leak on My Motorcycle?
  A motorcycle oil leak is its own decision, not the car oil-leak page in
  different words: the engine sits in the airstream, the chain and the rear tire
  are directly below, and there is no undertray catching anything. Separate a
  weep that leaves a film, a drip that marks the garage floor overnight, oil
  visible on the swingarm or rear wheel, and oil near the exhaust header.

Can I Ignore a Motorcycle That Wobbles at Speed?
  Distinguish the wobble that appears in a narrow speed band, the one that only
  shows up hands-off, the one that starts with luggage or a pillion, and the
  slow high-speed weave. Tire pressure, tire wear pattern, wheel balance,
  steering head bearings and loose axle or fork clamps all produce it and are
  worth telling apart.

Can I Ignore a Motorcycle That Pops on Deceleration?
  Exhaust popping or crackling on a closed throttle. Separate the light popping
  that many stock and de-catted bikes do by design, popping that appeared after
  an exhaust or air filter change, popping that arrived with poor running or
  hard starting, and loud backfiring with a bang. Fuelling, an air leak and a
  lean map are the usual mechanisms.

Can I Ignore a Slipping Motorcycle Clutch?
  Engine revs rising without matching acceleration. Separate slip only in top
  gear under hard acceleration, slip in every gear, slip that appeared right
  after an oil change, and a clutch that drags rather than slips. Friction plate
  wear, incorrect free play and the wrong oil specification are different fixes.

Can I Ignore a Motorcycle Battery That Keeps Dying?
  Separate a bike ridden only on short trips, a bike stored for weeks without a
  tender, a battery that is several seasons old, and a parasitic draw or a
  charging fault that flattens a good battery in days. Lead-acid and lithium
  batteries behave differently in cold storage.

Can I Ignore a Bike Chain That Keeps Skipping?
  A chain that jumps under load, usually when standing on the pedals. Separate
  skipping in one gear only, skipping across the whole cassette, skipping that
  started after a new chain went onto an old cassette, and a stiff or seized
  link. Chain wear measurement is the concrete thing a reader can check.

Can I Ignore Bike Brakes That Keep Rubbing?
  Both rim and disc. Separate a disc rotor that ticks once per wheel revolution,
  constant rub through the whole rotation, rub that appears only when pedalling
  hard, and a pad that drags after a wheel was refitted. Worth saying what the
  rub costs and what it does to pads and rotor over time.

Can I Ignore a Wobbly Bike Wheel?
  Lateral wobble seen from above while the wheel spins. Separate a small wobble
  that clears the brake, a wobble that hits the pad each revolution, a wobble
  that appeared after a pothole, and a wobble with a spoke that is obviously
  loose or broken. Rim braking and disc braking change what the wobble affects.

Can I Ignore a Clicking Noise When I Pedal?
  A click that repeats once per pedal stroke, not per wheel revolution. Separate
  a click only under load, a click on one side, one that changes when standing,
  and one that appeared after a component was fitted. Pedals, cleats, bottom
  bracket, chainring bolts, saddle rails and seatpost all click.

Can I Ignore Cracks in a Bike Tire?
  Separate fine crazing across the sidewall from age and UV, cracks in the tread
  base between knobs, a cut deep enough to show casing threads, and a bulge in
  the sidewall. Storage conditions, tire age and pressure history matter here.

Can I Ignore an E-Bike Battery That Gets Hot While Charging?
  Warm and hot are different things and the page should say where the line sits
  according to the manufacturer, not to a made-up number. Separate a pack that
  is warm to the touch at the end of a charge, one that is too hot to hold, one
  that smells, one that hisses or swells, and one being charged with a charger
  that did not come with the bike. Lithium-ion fire guidance from safety
  regulators is directly on point.

Can I Ignore an E-Bike That Cuts Out While Riding?
  Motor assist dropping out mid-ride. Separate a cut-out over bumps, a cut-out
  under full load on a climb, a cut-out that comes back after a restart, one
  that is accompanied by a display error code, and one that happens with a
  battery near empty or very cold. Connectors, battery mount, controller
  temperature and low-voltage cut-off are different causes.

Can I Ignore a Sudden Drop in E-Bike Battery Range?
  Distinguish a gradual seasonal drop, a drop after cold weather arrived, a drop
  after a long period of storage at full charge, and a genuinely sudden loss of
  half the range in a week. Also separate range loss from a display or state-of-
  charge reading that is simply wrong.

Can I Ignore an E-Bike Battery That Got Wet?
  Separate riding in rain with the pack fitted as designed, a pack left out in a
  downpour off the bike, a pack that went into a puddle or was jet-washed, and a
  pack that was submerged. Whether it was charged afterwards matters. Look for
  the manufacturers' and safety regulators' guidance on wet lithium-ion packs.

Can I Ignore a Loose Electric Scooter Stem?
  Play in the folding stem or clamp on a standing scooter. Separate a small
  amount of fore-aft play, play that grows over a week, a rattle at the folding
  latch, and play with a visible crack or an already-adjusted clamp. Stem
  failure at speed is the mechanism worth explaining.

Can I Ignore Weak Brakes on an Electric Scooter?
  Separate brakes that were always weak on a cheap machine, brakes that have got
  worse, a lever that reaches the bar, regenerative braking alone with no
  working mechanical brake, and brakes that are weak only in the wet. Stopping
  distance requirements in scooter safety standards are the reference.

Can I Ignore an Electric Scooter Tire That Keeps Losing Air?
  Pneumatic tires only for the loss-of-air question, but the page can say where
  solid tires sit. Separate a tire topped up once a month, once a week, one that
  is flat by the next morning, and one losing air with a visible object in the
  tread. Low pressure on small wheels changes handling and pinch-flat risk.
